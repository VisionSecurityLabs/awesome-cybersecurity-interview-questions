## Mid-Level

### Q1. How do you handle pushback from developers when enforcing security requirements?

*Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ &nbsp;|&nbsp; Format: behavioral*

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer covers</summary>

- Understands developer perspective
- Explains the why, not just the what
- Offers solutions, not just problems
- Finds compromise where appropriate
- Builds relationships proactively

</details>

## Senior / Advanced

### Q1. You are the security lead on an unreleased acquisition deal. You receive a compelling offer from the acquiring company. What are your obligations, and how do you handle the situation?

*Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ &nbsp;|&nbsp; Format: scenario*

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer covers</summary>

- Immediately recognizes the conflict of interest and does not delay addressing it
- Discloses the offer to their manager or legal counsel before making any decision
- Does not share any deal-related information with the prospective employer
- Considers whether they need to recuse themselves from further work on the deal
- Reviews their NDA and employment agreement before taking any action
- Understands that accepting the role may require a clean handoff and waiting period
- Does not rationalize sharing information as harmless because "the deal will be public soon"
- Recognizes that handling this poorly has legal and reputational consequences beyond this job

</details>

## All Levels

### Q1. The threat landscape changes constantly. How do you stay up to date?

*Difficulty: ⭐⭐ &nbsp;|&nbsp; Format: behavioral*

<details>
<summary>What a strong answer covers</summary>

- Follow security news sources (Twitter, blogs, newsletters)
- Participate in communities (conferences, meetups, Slack/Discord)
- Hands-on learning (labs, CTFs, certifications)
- Learn from incidents (post-mortems, case studies)
- Vendor threat intelligence reports
- Allocate dedicated learning time

</details>
